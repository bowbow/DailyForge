# Creativity Program v0.1

Status: Draft for user review

Date: 2026-10-31

Evidence base: [Creativity challenge research](Creativity_research.md)

## Purpose

This document turns the Creativity research into an initial Daily Forge program.
It is a practical starting point, not a fixed curriculum. The program should
change as the user makes work, reflects on it, and discovers which challenges
are useful.

The primary medium is **digital painting in Rebelle 7 Pro**. Gaea and Unreal
Engine are optional supporting tools for terrain, viewpoint, lighting, and
reference creation. Occasional non-painting Spark challenges are allowed when
they protect play or provoke a useful visual idea, but painting remains the
program's main drive.

The default challenge duration is **15 minutes**. A longer painting develops
through connected 15-minute studio episodes. Completing an episode means doing
the bounded work sincerely; it does not require finishing the painting.

## Major quest

> Create atmospheric, mountain-dominant digital landscapes with intentional
> warm and cool color relationships, using J. M. W. Turner as an important
> source of study while developing an individual visual voice.

This is a user-controlled artistic quest. It does not require another person to
approve, exhibit, praise, or buy the work.

## Program principles

1. **Studio Thinking is primary.** Develop Craft, Engage & Persist, Envision,
   Express, Observe, Reflect, Stretch & Explore, and Understand Art Worlds are
   non-hierarchical habits that interact while making art.
2. **Paintings begin early.** The user does not complete a technical syllabus
   before attempting meaningful work. Active work reveals what should be
   learned next.
3. **Craft is embedded.** A technical challenge names an artistic purpose,
   teaches only the needed operation, and applies it immediately.
4. **The cycle is recursive.** Brief instruction, sustained making, private
   critique, revision, intentional stopping, and presentation can recur in any
   useful order.
5. **The user remains the artist.** The app can focus attention and offer
   alternatives; it cannot determine meaning, taste, quality, or completion.
6. **Tools remain subordinate.** Gaea and Unreal serve visual questions. A
   technically elaborate scene is not evidence of a better painting.
7. **Play is protected.** Some challenges can be purposeless, disposable,
   strange, or unrelated to the major quest.
8. **Completion is supportive.** Partial progress, deliberate pausing, and
   intentional abandonment remain part of the private record without shame.

## Goals

The goals are controllable outcomes and practices, not scores or a staircase.
They may develop together.

### CG-01 — Make atmospheric mountain landscapes

Create original digital landscapes in which scale, depth, weather, light, and
atmosphere are deliberate painting decisions rather than incidental effects.

**Evidence of progress:** preserved studies and paintings, increasingly
specific reflections, and the ability to identify which choices create or
weaken atmosphere.

### CG-02 — Use warm and cool color relationships intentionally

Learn to observe, mix, organize, and revise warm/cool relationships so they
support light, depth, unity, emphasis, and feeling.

**Evidence of progress:** temperature maps, palette studies, paired variants,
and paintings in which the user can explain the intended warm/cool structure.

### CG-03 — Learn from Turner and transfer the learning

Study legally usable Turner references through observation, component studies,
credited master studies, memory, and reinterpretation. Transfer selected
principles into original landscapes rather than trying to reproduce a fixed
surface style indefinitely.

**Evidence of progress:** attributed studies, specific observations about
value, temperature, edges, atmosphere, movement, and evidence of those ideas
being transformed in original work.

### CG-04 — Sustain, revise, and finish selected paintings

Keep meaningful work active across multiple sessions. Use private critique to
decide whether to persist, experiment, revise, pause, abandon deliberately, or
declare a work finished.

**Evidence of progress:** version history, recorded decisions, completed or
intentionally closed works, and selected paintings prepared for private review
or presentation.

### CG-05 — Use the full digital landscape pipeline when it serves the work

Use Gaea → Unreal → Rebelle to explore terrain, viewpoint, light, and painting
interpretation without making mastery of Gaea or Unreal an independent goal.

**Evidence of progress:** completed handoffs, painted interpretations that
depart from their renders, and clear knowledge of which pipeline steps help or
distract.

### CG-06 — Preserve play and creative range

Continue making low-stakes experiments that allow curiosity, accidents,
exaggeration, and unrelated forms of creativity.

**Evidence of progress:** participation and honest reflection about enjoyment
or discovery. Artifact quality and retention are irrelevant.

## How a painting develops

A painting does not have to follow these states in order. They give the app a
shared vocabulary for choosing a useful next challenge:

- **Question:** the feeling, visual problem, or inquiry the work explores.
- **Possibilities:** thumbnails, references, palettes, compositions, and mental
  alternatives.
- **Making:** active construction and consequential painting decisions.
- **Sticking point:** a named perceptual, expressive, or technical obstacle.
- **Critique:** comparison of intention and current effect using visible
  evidence.
- **Revision:** a bounded change with a stated purpose.
- **Closure decision:** continue, pause, abandon deliberately, or finish.
- **Presentation:** select, export, document, contextualize, show, or submit.

The app should allow movement in any direction. A critique may return the user
to possibilities; play may create a new question; presentation may expose a
needed revision.

## Challenge model

Every Creativity challenge should define:

- a stable identifier and title;
- lane: Spark, Practice, Project, Reflection, or Presentation;
- one primary Studio Habit and zero to three supporting habits;
- studio structure: brief instruction, work, critique, or exhibition;
- related goal and optional active work;
- artistic purpose in plain language;
- a bounded 15-minute prompt;
- tools and saved inputs required;
- completion condition based on participation or a decision;
- one optional private reflection question;
- an easier or tool-light fallback when appropriate; and
- an optional continuation, never an automatic escalation.

A challenge should not try to exercise all eight habits. The program creates
breadth across time while allowing several habits to interact naturally.

## Selection policy

The next challenge should respond to the user's declared state:

- **No active work:** offer an expressive seed, observation study, or playful
  Spark.
- **Clear intention, no start:** offer Envision or a small first Making episode.
- **Active work with momentum:** offer a bounded Project continuation.
- **Named craft obstacle:** offer a brief Develop Craft demonstration followed
  by immediate use.
- **Unclear next step:** offer private Reflect critique before prescribing a
  technique.
- **Stuck or repetitive:** offer Stretch & Explore variants or a change of
  constraint.
- **Possible completion:** offer a closure critique, then respect the user's
  decision.
- **Ready for an audience:** offer documentation, selection, contextualization,
  or submission work.
- **Pipeline active:** offer the next useful handoff episode, but allow a return
  to painting at any point.

Habit history may prompt a suggestion when an important habit has been absent,
but must not enforce a quota. The user can replace a challenge without penalty.

## Starter studio cycle

This is a recommended first cycle, not a mandatory sequence. It collects a
baseline while beginning meaningful work immediately. Each episode is 15
minutes.

| ID       | Challenge                      | Primary habit     | Studio structure | Prompt and completion                                                                                                                              |
| -------- | ------------------------------ | ----------------- | ---------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| CR-P-001 | Mountain atmosphere baseline   | Express           | Work             | In Rebelle, paint a mountain landscape from memory whose intended feeling is immensity. Stop at 15 minutes and save it.                            |
| CR-R-001 | Intention versus effect        | Reflect           | Critique         | View the baseline at thumbnail size. Record what currently creates immensity, what weakens it, and one next experiment.                            |
| CR-P-002 | Turner temperature map         | Observe           | Work             | From one credited, legally usable Turner reference, make a simple map of its largest warm, cool, and neutral regions.                              |
| CR-P-003 | Warm/cool mixing strip         | Develop Craft     | Brief + Work     | In Rebelle, mix a small warm-to-cool family suitable for one sky and one mountain shadow; apply two mixtures to a tiny test.                       |
| CR-J-001 | Apply one temperature decision | Engage & Persist  | Work             | Reopen the baseline and make only the warm/cool change selected during critique. Save a new version.                                               |
| CR-S-001 | Impossible mountain weather    | Stretch & Explore | Work             | Invent an impossible weather condition and make a disposable color-and-mark response. Plausibility is not required.                                |
| CR-R-002 | Compare without ranking        | Reflect           | Critique         | Compare the two baseline versions. Name what each version does differently; choose one idea to carry forward without declaring a universal winner. |
| CR-J-002 | Begin an intentional landscape | Envision          | Work             | Make three tiny mountain thumbnails for a chosen feeling, select one, and write one sentence describing its intended effect.                       |

After this cycle, selection should follow the active work rather than continue
through a predetermined list.

## Fifteen-minute challenge rules

- The timer is a boundary, not a productivity test.
- Setup time counts when setup is necessary to do the work.
- The user may stop early for fatigue, frustration, accessibility, or loss of
  relevance and record what happened without penalty.
- An active painting can receive many 15-minute episodes.
- The app must not imply that every painting should be finishable in 15 minutes.
- A challenge can offer an optional continuation, but continuation does not
  increase credit or preserve a streak more strongly.
- When a technical obstacle consumes the session, identifying and recording the
  obstacle can count; the next challenge may address it briefly.

## Private critique prompts

Critique should be evidence-based and non-judgmental. Useful prompts include:

- What should the viewer notice or feel first?
- What visible choices currently support that effect?
- What competes with or weakens it?
- What should remain untouched?
- Which single experiment would provide the most information?
- Is the next change likely to clarify the work or merely polish it?
- Should this work continue, pause, be abandoned deliberately, or finish?

The app should not assign a quality score or use an artifact to infer talent.

## Initial program data

The first challenges can collect the remaining information through making:

- installed Rebelle 7 Pro point release;
- input device, display, pressure, color, performance, and ergonomic constraints;
- preferred simulated media and brushes;
- installed Gaea and Unreal versions and whether their handoff currently works;
- current unfinished works;
- preferred atmosphere, landscape references, and Turner works;
- what “finished” means for a study, standalone painting, portfolio work, and
  submission-ready work;
- reactions to 15-minute duration; and
- which prompts feel energizing, neutral, frustrating, or irrelevant.

Unknown data should cause a probe or a reversible default, not block the whole
program.

## Implementation status

The initial in-memory application slice now includes:

- the major quest, six goals, Studio Thinking explanation, tool roles, and
  15-minute model in the Creativity area guide;
- all eight starter-cycle challenges in the development catalog;
- versioned lane, habit, Studio Structure, goal, reflection-prompt, and
  active-work metadata in domain and API contracts;
- an active mountain-atmosphere work that can be continued, paused, or finished
  independently of challenge completion and streaks;
- contextual metadata on Today cards and completed Creativity wall entries; and
- automated domain, contract, API, desktop, mobile, and accessibility coverage.

Automatic challenge selection is not yet implemented. Development replacement
cycles through the reviewed starter catalog deterministically. Later work can
add user-created active works, richer critique records, durable persistence, and
selection rules after the first cycle has been used and reviewed in practice.
